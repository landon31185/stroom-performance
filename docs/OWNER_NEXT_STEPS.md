# What We Need From You To Launch Stroom Online

Hi Jake,

The website foundation is ready. The next step is turning it from a premium
catalog preview into a real store where customers can safely buy the parts you
actually want to sell.

You do **not** need to build web pages or touch code. We need your product and
business facts so we can set up Shopify correctly and make sure customers see
accurate prices, fitment, shipping, and availability.

## 1. Set up Shopify access

1. Create the Stroom Shopify store using the business email you want to own it
   under, or send us access to an existing store.
2. Invite us as a collaborator/staff member so we can configure the storefront
   and connect it to the existing website.
3. Send us the Shopify store URL once it exists.

Please do not spend time choosing a Shopify theme or installing apps yet. The
custom website is already built; Shopify will manage the products, inventory,
orders, and checkout behind it.

## 2. Send the first product batch

Start with one product if that is easier. For every part you want to sell, send
the following information. Copy and fill out this block once per product:

```text
Product name:
Brand:
Manufacturer part number:
Stroom SKU, if different:
Supplier / where Stroom will buy it:

Retail price:
Any MAP or pricing restriction:
Available options: size, color, engine type, finish, etc.:
Supplier SKU for each option:
Current availability or typical lead time:

What engine/platform does it fit?
What must a customer confirm before ordering?
What does it not fit?
Source link, manufacturer documentation, or notes supporting fitment:

Shipping weight and package dimensions:
Where it ships from:
Warranty details:
Return restrictions, if any:

Approved product photos or a folder link:
Who owns/approved those photos for Stroom to use:
```

The first four reference products already have page templates waiting for this
information: PAC valve springs, BTR turbo cam, BTR intake manifold, and Holley
Terminator X. They are intentionally not listed as purchasable until we have
the real price, supplier, shipping, and approval details.

## 3. Confirm the business rules

Please answer these once for the store:

- What states or countries will you ship to at launch?
- Will shipping be free, flat-rate, calculated at checkout, or quoted later?
- What is the return window and are there any non-returnable items?
- What warranty wording should customers see?
- What support email and phone number should customers use?
- What business name/address should appear in store policies and receipts?
- What is the final domain you want customers to use?
- For drop shipping: how does an order get sent to the supplier, how is tracking
  returned, and where are returns sent?

## 4. Confirm the photos we already have

We have build, engine-bay, cockpit, and track photos saved for future use.
Please tell us:

- Which photos you approve for public use on the website.
- What vehicle/build each photo shows.
- Which parts shown are relevant enough to mention.
- Whether anyone visible in a photo is okay with the image being used publicly.

Those photos are best used to show real build experience and track context.
For product pages, we still need clean photos of the exact product customers
will receive.

## What We Will Do Once We Have This

1. Configure Shopify and connect it to the existing website.
2. Add the first products as drafts and make sure product facts, fitment, media,
   cart, and checkout work correctly.
3. Test a real order from product page through payment and fulfillment handoff.
4. Publish only after pricing, inventory, shipping, policies, and customer
   support information are accurate.

Our first goal is simple: launch one correct, shoppable product end to end.
Once that works, adding the rest of the catalog becomes a repeatable process.
